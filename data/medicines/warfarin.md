# Warfarin

Warfarin is a drug based on the active ingredient Warfarin Sodium, belonging to the category of Anticoagulants and specifically Vitamin K antagonists.

## How to use Warfarin

- Adults: dose is strictly individual and determined by regular INR (International Normalized Ratio) monitoring. The typical starting dose is 2–5 mg once daily, taken at the same time each day, preferably in the evening. Dose adjustments are made based on INR results. Never change the dose without medical supervision.
- Take with or without food, but maintain a consistent dietary routine — especially regarding Vitamin K intake (green leafy vegetables such as spinach, kale, broccoli).
- Missed doses should be taken as soon as remembered on the same day. Do not double the dose the following day.

## When Warfarin should not be used

- Hypersensitivity to Warfarin Sodium or any excipient.
- Active clinically significant bleeding or hemorrhagic tendencies (e.g. haemophilia, thrombocytopenia).
- Recent or planned surgery on the central nervous system, eye, or traumatic surgery.
- Severe hepatic impairment or liver disease affecting coagulation.
- Pregnancy (first and third trimester) — teratogenic and risk of fetal hemorrhage.
- Uncontrolled severe hypertension.
- Concomitant use of NSAIDs (including Ibuprofen, Aspirin, Naproxen) significantly increases bleeding risk and is generally contraindicated without close medical supervision.

## Important interactions

- **NSAIDs (Ibuprofen, Aspirin, Naproxen, Diclofenac):** strongly potentiate the anticoagulant effect and increase gastrointestinal bleeding risk. Combination should be avoided or used only under strict INR monitoring.
- **Antibiotics:** many antibiotics alter gut flora and increase INR unpredictably.
- **Vitamin K-rich foods:** large or variable intake of green leafy vegetables reduces Warfarin effectiveness.
- **Paracetamol (Acetaminophen):** regular use at doses above 2g/day may increase INR.
- **Alcohol:** moderate-to-heavy consumption increases bleeding risk and INR variability.

## Special precautions

- Regular INR monitoring is mandatory throughout therapy.
- Patients should carry an anticoagulant alert card.
- Any sign of unusual bleeding (bruising, prolonged bleeding from cuts, blood in urine or stool, unusual headache) should be reported immediately.
- Renal impairment: use with caution; reduced renal clearance may increase bleeding risk.

# Metformin

Metformin is a drug based on the active ingredient Metformin Hydrochloride, belonging to the category of Antidiabetics and specifically Biguanides.

## How to use Metformin

- Adults: initial dose is typically 500 mg or 850 mg once or twice daily with meals. Dose is increased gradually (every 10–15 days) to reduce gastrointestinal side effects. Maintenance dose: 1500–2000 mg/day in 2–3 divided doses. Maximum dose: 3000 mg/day.
- **Must be taken with or immediately after meals** to minimise gastrointestinal side effects (nausea, diarrhoea, abdominal discomfort). This is particularly important when combined with Levodopa, which requires the opposite timing (before meals) — careful scheduling is required.
- Extended-release formulations (Metformin XR) are taken once daily with the evening meal and may have better gastrointestinal tolerability.
- Do not crush extended-release tablets.

## When Metformin should not be used

- Hypersensitivity to Metformin Hydrochloride or any excipient.
- **Renal impairment:** contraindicated if eGFR < 30 mL/min. Use with caution if eGFR 30–45 mL/min (dose reduction required). Risk of Metformin-associated lactic acidosis increases with declining renal function.
- Acute or chronic conditions that may impair renal function: dehydration, severe infection, shock, iodinated contrast media administration (temporary discontinuation required).
- Hepatic impairment: avoid in severe hepatic disease due to increased risk of lactic acidosis.
- Acute or chronic alcohol abuse.
- Diabetic ketoacidosis or diabetic pre-coma.
- Heart failure requiring pharmacological treatment.

## Important interactions

- **Ibuprofen and other NSAIDs:** can reduce renal perfusion, increasing the risk of renal impairment and consequently Metformin-associated lactic acidosis. Avoid combination, especially in elderly patients or those with pre-existing renal disease.
- **Iodinated contrast agents:** Metformin must be temporarily discontinued before and after procedures using iodinated contrast due to risk of acute renal failure and lactic acidosis.
- **Alcohol:** potentiates the risk of lactic acidosis.
- **Corticosteroids:** may increase blood glucose levels, requiring dose adjustment.
- **Diuretics (especially loop diuretics):** may impair renal function and increase lactic acidosis risk.
- **ACE inhibitors / ARBs:** may reduce renal function in susceptible patients; monitor closely.
- **Levodopa:** no direct pharmacokinetic interaction, but concurrent use in Parkinson's patients with Diabetes requires careful meal-timing planning, as Levodopa must be taken away from meals while Metformin must be taken with meals.

## Special precautions

- Renal function (eGFR) must be checked before starting treatment and monitored at least annually, or more frequently in elderly patients or those at risk.
- Lactic acidosis is a rare but serious adverse effect — risk factors include renal impairment, hepatic disease, alcohol use, dehydration, and use of iodinated contrast.
- Withhold Metformin temporarily during acute illness associated with dehydration, severe infection, or surgery.
- Vitamin B12 absorption may be reduced with long-term use; periodic monitoring is recommended.
- In patients with Parkinson's disease: coordinate meal and medication timing carefully with the Levodopa schedule to avoid pharmacokinetic conflicts.

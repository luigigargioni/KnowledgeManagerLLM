# Sitagliptin
Sitagliptin is a drug based on the active ingredient Sitagliptin Phosphate, belonging to the category of Antidiabetic agents and specifically DPP-4 inhibitors (gliptins). It is commonly used as an alternative to Metformin in patients with renal impairment, since its dose can be adjusted according to kidney function.
## What Sitagliptin is used for
Sitagliptin is used to lower blood glucose levels in patients with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus, either alone or in combination with other antidiabetic agents, and is particularly useful when Metformin cannot be used due to reduced kidney function.
## How to use Sitagliptin
- Adults: 100 mg once daily, with dose reduction to 50 mg or 25 mg once daily depending on the degree of renal impairment, with or without food.
## When Sitagliptin should not be used
- hypersensitivity to the active ingredient or to any of the excipients;
- history of severe hypersensitivity reactions to other DPP-4 inhibitors;
- type 1 diabetes mellitus or diabetic ketoacidosis;
- history of pancreatitis.

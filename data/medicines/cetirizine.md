# Cetirizine
Cetirizine is a drug based on the active ingredient Cetirizine Dihydrochloride, belonging to the category of Antihistamines and specifically second-generation, non-sedating H1-receptor antagonists.
## What Cetirizine is used for
Cetirizine is used to relieve the symptoms of Seasonal Allergies and allergic rhinitis (sneezing, runny nose, itchy and watery eyes) and of chronic urticaria. As an antihistamine it is often taken in the morning, before the daily exposure to pollen and other airborne allergens.
## How to use Cetirizine
- Adults: 10 mg once a day, taken after breakfast; the effect is reached within about 20-60 minutes of the dose and lasts 24 hours.
## When Cetirizine should not be used
- hypersensitivity to the active ingredient, to hydroxyzine, to piperazine derivatives or to any of the excipients;
- end-stage renal disease with creatinine clearance below 10 ml/min.
## Cautions and monitoring
- Chronic Kidney Disease: halve the dose in moderate to severe renal impairment, since Cetirizine is eliminated largely unchanged by the kidney.
- Mild Asthma: no contraindication. Cetirizine treats the allergic component and does not relieve bronchospasm; it is not a substitute for a rescue bronchodilator such as Salbutamol.
- Drowsiness is uncommon but possible; in elderly patients or patients with dementia it may add to confusion, so pair it with supervision rather than with unassisted activity.
- Alcohol and other central nervous system depressants increase the sedative effect.

# Levodopa

Levodopa is a drug based on the active ingredient Levodopa (usually combined with Carbidopa or Benserazide as a decarboxylase inhibitor), belonging to the category of Antiparkinsonian agents and specifically Dopamine precursors.

## How to use Levodopa

- Adults: dose is strictly individual and titrated gradually under neurological supervision. A typical starting dose is 50–100 mg of Levodopa (with decarboxylase inhibitor) 2–3 times daily, increasing slowly over weeks.
- **Timing relative to meals is critical:** Levodopa absorption is significantly reduced by dietary protein. It should be taken at least 30 minutes before meals or 60–90 minutes after meals for optimal absorption. High-protein meals can substantially reduce effectiveness.
- Do not crush or chew modified-release formulations.
- Do not discontinue abruptly — sudden withdrawal can cause a severe reaction (Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome-like state).
- Administer at consistent times daily to maintain stable plasma levels and minimise motor fluctuations ("on-off" phenomena).

## When Levodopa should not be used

- Hypersensitivity to Levodopa, Carbidopa, Benserazide, or any excipient.
- Narrow-angle glaucoma (uncontrolled).
- Severe psychosis or history of psychotic episodes not controlled by medication.
- Concomitant use with non-selective MAO inhibitors (must be discontinued at least 14 days before starting Levodopa).
- Malignant melanoma or history thereof (Levodopa may activate melanocytes).
- Severe cardiac arrhythmias or recent myocardial infarction.

## Important interactions

- **Dietary protein:** large protein meals reduce Levodopa intestinal absorption significantly. Protein redistribution diets (low protein during the day) are sometimes recommended for advanced Parkinson's patients.
- **Antipsychotics (Haloperidol, Risperidone, Quetiapine):** dopamine receptor antagonists reduce Levodopa effectiveness; may worsen Parkinsonism. If antipsychotics are necessary, Quetiapine or Clozapine are preferred.
- **Iron supplements:** reduce Levodopa absorption — administer at least 2 hours apart.
- **Metoclopramide:** reduces gastric emptying and Levodopa absorption; also a dopamine antagonist.
- **Antihypertensives:** additive hypotensive effect, especially orthostatic hypotension.
- **Metformin:** no direct pharmacokinetic interaction; however, both drugs require careful scheduling in patients with Parkinson's and Diabetes to avoid postprandial glucose spikes and meal-timing conflicts with Levodopa absorption.

## Special precautions

- Orthostatic hypotension is common, especially at initiation — advise patients to rise slowly from sitting or lying positions. Increases fall risk in elderly patients.
- Monitor for neuropsychiatric symptoms: hallucinations, impulse control disorders, confusion (particularly in elderly patients).
- Dyskinesias (involuntary movements) may develop after prolonged use.
- Periodic cardiovascular, hepatic, renal, and haematopoietic monitoring is recommended during long-term therapy.
- In patients with Parkinson's disease and Depression: some antidepressants (especially tricyclics and MAOIs) interact dangerously with Levodopa. SSRIs are generally preferred but should still be used with caution.

# Ibuprofen

Ibuprofen is a drug based on the active ingredient Ibuprofen, belonging to the category of Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs) and specifically Propionic acid derivatives.

## How to use Ibuprofen

- Adults: 200–400 mg per dose, up to 3 times daily. The minimum effective dose should be used for the shortest duration necessary. Maximum daily dose: 1200 mg (OTC); up to 2400 mg under medical supervision.
- Take with food, milk, or a full glass of water to reduce gastrointestinal irritation.
- Do not use for more than 10 consecutive days for pain or 3 days for fever without medical advice.
- Elderly patients: use the lowest effective dose for the shortest possible time due to increased risk of gastrointestinal and renal adverse effects.

## When Ibuprofen should not be used

- Hypersensitivity to Ibuprofen or other NSAIDs (risk of cross-reactivity with Aspirin and other salicylates).
- History of asthma, urticaria, or allergic reactions triggered by Aspirin or other NSAIDs.
- Active or history of peptic ulcer disease or gastrointestinal bleeding.
- Severe hepatic impairment.
- **Severe or moderate renal impairment (eGFR < 60 mL/min):** NSAIDs reduce renal perfusion and can precipitate acute kidney injury. Contraindicated in chronic kidney disease stage 3 and above.
- Heart failure, established ischemic heart disease, peripheral arterial disease, or cerebrovascular disease.
- Concomitant use with anticoagulants such as **Warfarin:** significantly increases the risk of serious and potentially fatal bleeding. This combination requires strict medical supervision and is generally contraindicated in elderly patients.
- Third trimester of pregnancy.

## Important interactions

- **Warfarin and anticoagulants:** potentiates anticoagulant effect; greatly increases hemorrhagic risk. Avoid combination whenever possible.
- **Antihypertensives (ACE inhibitors, ARBs, beta-blockers, diuretics):** NSAIDs may blunt antihypertensive effects and increase risk of renal impairment.
- **Metformin:** concurrent use in patients with renal impairment may increase the risk of Metformin-associated lactic acidosis due to NSAID-induced renal function deterioration.
- **Corticosteroids:** increased risk of gastrointestinal ulceration and bleeding.
- **SSRIs / SNRIs:** increased risk of gastrointestinal bleeding.
- **Lithium:** NSAIDs reduce renal lithium clearance, increasing plasma levels and toxicity risk.

## Special precautions

- Elderly patients are at significantly higher risk of serious gastrointestinal, cardiovascular, and renal adverse events.
- Chronic kidney disease: even short-term use can cause acute deterioration of renal function.
- Cardiovascular risk: NSAIDs may increase the risk of serious thrombotic events, myocardial infarction and stroke.
- Monitor renal function, blood pressure, and signs of fluid retention during prolonged use.

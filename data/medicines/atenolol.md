# Atenolol
Atenolol is a drug based on the active ingredient Atenolol, belonging to the category of Cardiovascular agents and specifically Cardioselective (beta-1 selective) Beta-blockers.
## What Atenolol is used for
Atenolol is used to treat Hypertension, angina pectoris, certain arrhythmias and palpitations, and to reduce heart rate and cardiac workload after a myocardial infarction.
## How to use Atenolol
- Adults: 25-50 mg once a day, adjusted according to response, up to a maximum of 100 mg per day, taken in the morning after breakfast.
## When Atenolol should not be used
- hypersensitivity to the active ingredient or to any of the excipients;
- severe bradycardia, sick sinus syndrome, or second/third-degree atrioventricular block;
- decompensated heart failure or cardiogenic shock;
- untreated pheochromocytoma;
- metabolic acidosis.
## Cautions and monitoring
- Mild Asthma and other reversible obstructive airway disease: Atenolol is beta-1 selective and is not absolutely contraindicated, unlike non-selective beta-blockers such as Propranolol, but selectivity is dose-dependent and bronchospasm remains possible. Use the lowest effective dose, keep a rescue bronchodilator available, and monitor for wheeze or shortness of breath after the dose.
- Diabetes: may mask the warning signs of hypoglycaemia, in particular tachycardia and tremor.
- Renal impairment: Atenolol is eliminated by the kidney, so the dose must be reduced in Chronic Kidney Disease.
- Never stop the treatment abruptly: withdrawal can cause rebound angina or arrhythmia.

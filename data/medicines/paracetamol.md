# Paracetamol
Paracetamol (Acetaminophen) is a drug based on the active ingredient Paracetamol, belonging to the category of Analgesics and Antipyretics, and specifically Anilides. It is often used as a safer alternative to NSAIDs such as Aspirin or Ibuprofen in patients with renal, cardiac, or gastrointestinal contraindications.
## What Paracetamol is used for
Paracetamol is used to relieve mild to moderate pain and to reduce fever. It is generally the preferred first-line option for pain or fever management in patients with kidney disease, heart failure, or a history of gastrointestinal bleeding, since it lacks the anti-platelet and gastric-irritant effects of NSAIDs.
## How to use Paracetamol
- Adults: 500-1000 mg every 4-6 hours as needed, not exceeding 3000-4000 mg per day. Can be taken with or without food.
## When Paracetamol should not be used
- hypersensitivity to the active ingredient or to any of the excipients;
- severe hepatic insufficiency or active liver disease;
- chronic alcohol abuse, due to increased risk of liver toxicity.
